# Results and source audit

The earlier literature collection has 47 MIC records across nine studies. The 2014 L-type study also supplies a separate 242-isolate spot sheet. In that sheet, 150 isolates are P. aeruginosa and 92 are other pseudomonads. The raw ABR field is numeric for 70 of the 150 P. aeruginosa isolates and NT for 80. T remains undefined in the source sheet, so every L-type response result retains both coding rules.

Laosena et al. (2026) Data S1 supplies 29 isolates, six pyocins, and three inhibition-zone readings per protein-isolate pair. All 174 triplets agreed on zero versus nonzero status. The same supplement reports three MIC columns with 73 numeric entries and 14 NC entries, four biofilm-formation readings on each isolate, and treated-biofilm readings for three selected high-forming isolates. These are spot-dilution MICs, so they were not pooled with earlier broth MICs. The six reference accessions were taken from the authors' Table S2 and fetched from NCBI Protein. The source workbook SHA-256 is e47feae489f87a06b49ec6b49e71b0be71d7c57d6d1eda0780d6e7387b8d97a1.

The observed six-protein length-coverage Spearman coefficient is 0.886. Its two-sided exact permutation P value is 0.033 across 720 label permutations. This is exploratory: the six proteins differ in sequence and functional class, and the activity rank is selected from one 29-isolate panel. Leave-one-protein-out coefficients range from 0.80 to 0.90. The mean-zone correlation has the same rank order and is not independent confirmation.

The paired numeric MIC comparison uses 17 isolates with all three values available. NC was not converted to a censoring threshold. Biofilm A405 is a metabolic signal, not survival or MIC. S6 receptor counts were transcribed from Dingemans et al. (2016) Table 3 and were not merged with another cohort. The original 2014 species and ABR analyses are descriptive reanalyses of previously published data.

Pairwise complete-case MIC comparisons add a more direct check. SX1 and SX2 share 75% Clustal Omega identity and the published source assigns both to CrtA uptake. Of 19 isolates with both numeric MICs, SX2 had the higher MIC in 14, SX1 in three, with two ties. Among 17 non-tied pairs, the higher-MIC fraction was 0.824 with an exact 95% binomial interval of 0.566 to 0.962. The median SX2/SX1 ratio was 4.01. The exact two-sided sign P value was 0.0127, or 0.0255 after Holm correction over all three pyocin pairs. For AP41 versus SX2, all 18 numeric pairs favoured AP41, the median SX2/AP41 ratio was 16.0, and the Holm-adjusted sign P value was 0.0000229. These comparisons concern only the reported numeric subsets; the missing or NC cells were never converted into numeric MICs. Zone-response discordance for SX1 versus SX2 was seven versus one, with a Holm-adjusted exact paired P value of 0.196. Zone coverage and MIC magnitude therefore remain separate conclusions.

Clustal Omega completed a six-protein alignment, archived in data with its service job ID. IUPred3 long mode subsequently completed for all six reference sequences. The archived raw score arrays contain one score per residue and match the accession-checked FASTA lengths. Predicted disordered fractions range from 0.073 for M1 to 0.340 for AP41. These are predictions, not measured structures.

Across the six proteins, predicted disorder fraction ranked with response breadth (Spearman rho = 0.943; exact two-sided permutation P = 0.0167). The corresponding length result was rho = 0.886, P = 0.0333. Screening six sequence features and applying Benjamini-Hochberg correction gives q = 0.10 for each of these two signals. Length and disorder fraction themselves have rho = 0.943, so the selected panel cannot tell which is responsible for the activity pattern. N-terminal 40-residue disorder, charge at pH 7, hydropathy, and aromaticity did not meet the same threshold. Clustal Omega percent identity and response-set Jaccard overlap across 15 protein pairs had rho = 0.568; an exact permutation of the six protein labels gave P = 0.108. The paired points are dependent, and no standard 15-pair P value was used.

InterProScan completed for all six accessions, and its raw TSV, JSON, GFF3, job ID, and parsed signature table are archived. The closest pair, SX1 and SX2, shared a Pfam PF06958 translocation annotation. In the Clustal Omega alignment, 135 of 140 residues in the SX1 PF06958 span matched SX2. In the terminal Gene3D DNase-annotated SX1 region, 19 of 103 paired columns matched SX2, with 27 additional SX2 gap columns. InterProScan did not call the corresponding DNase signature in SX2 at its thresholds. This is an annotation and alignment contrast, not evidence that the domain difference causes the MIC difference.
